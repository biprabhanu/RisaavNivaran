### Slide 4: Why agentic, not a checklist *(30 s)*

- **Chooses its next action:** expected information gain per rupee and per day
- **Re-plans:** an inspector becomes unavailable, so the action is excluded and the plan re-ranked

### Slide 5: Show-stoppers (USPs) *(40 s)*
- **Adaptive evidence planning:** score = EIG / (cost + λ·time). The records request (0.50 bits, ₹2,000, 1 day) beats the ground survey (0.91 bits, ₹60,000, 5 days)
- **Evidence-gated closure:** verified only if *post + uncertainty < pre − uncertainty*, with an independent measurement
- An operator repair claim alone **can never close a case**
- **Automatic reopening:** the 2026 observation reopens the case as RECURRING
- Humans approve attribution, notices, investigations and closure. The LLM only drafts text.

## 2. USP one-liners and elevator pitch

1. **"A repair claim is not a reduction."** PlumeProof refuses to close a case without an independent measurement.
2. **"The most information for the least rupee and day."** Investigations are ranked by expected information gain per cost and time.
3. **"It never forgets a leak."** Cases persist for months and reopen automatically when emissions return.

**15-second elevator pitch:**
"Only 13% of UN methane alerts ever get a response. PlumeProof is an AI agent that turns each satellite alert into a managed case. It picks the cheapest investigation that tells it the most, gets human sign-off, and refuses to close the case until an independent measurement proves the leak is fixed. Across 25 simulated scenarios: zero false closures."

## 3. The worked example in one breath

"We start with three suspects: the waste surface at 50%, the gas pipes at 35% and the flare at 15%. Our uncertainty is 1.44 bits; one bit is one fair coin-flip of doubt. For each investigation we ask how many bits it would remove on average: its expected information gain. Checking the operator's records removes about half a bit and costs ₹2,000 and one day. A full ground survey removes 0.91 bits but costs ₹60,000 and five days. Divide information by cost plus a rupee value for each day, and the records request wins by roughly ten times, so it ranks first. If the records show a gas-system fault, which we expect 43% of the time, Bayes' rule moves the gas pipes to 65%, the flare to 29% and the waste surface to just 6%."

## 4. Ten more tough judge questions

1. **"Isn't 0 false closures true by construction?"** Partly, yes, and we say so. The closure rule forbids closing without an independent verified reduction. What the evaluation tests is that the whole agent never bypasses that rule, even with missing records, conflicting measurements and false repair claims. It also measures the cost: more measurement campaigns. We also use a bounded measurement-noise model; with heavy-tailed noise a rare false "verified" would be possible.

2. **"Why does the ablation get the same accuracy as PlumeProof?"** Because it keeps the same stopping rule and verification gate; only the choice of next action changes. Picking cheapest-first starts with the free satellite wait, which costs nothing but takes 30 days and tells you nothing about the source. So the planner's value shows up as speed: 46.7 vs 78.8 days to closure, and 0.72 vs 0.84 source-ID inspections per case.

3. **"How did you pick λ?"** λ = ₹1,000 per day is an illustrative value for the cost of a day's delay. The Planner slider lets you sweep it from 0 to 20,000 live. The records request stays first across the default range, and at λ = 0 too. Choosing λ is a policy decision for the regulator, not something we tune.

4. **"What if bad actors falsify measurements?"** We require measurements to be *independent* (not operator-reported), we keep every measurement rather than the convenient one, and conflicting readings force INCONCLUSIVE. Each measurement is linked to its source in the audit log. We don't yet do cryptographic provenance or instrument certification; that would come from the regulator's accredited labs.

5. **"What would you need from MPCB / BMC to pilot this?"** Facility boundaries and the operator mapping, access to operational records (flare and gas-collection logs), inspector schedules, and a commitment to share follow-up measurements. Plus an environmental engineer to calibrate the likelihoods and costs. The software side runs today in a single container.

6. **"What does it cost to run?"** The core maths is microseconds per case and runs on a free Render instance with SQLite. The LLM is optional; our proposal budgets about ₹4,000 of API use for two months, and with no key the system uses templates. The real costs are the investigations it recommends, and minimising those is the point.

7. **"How is this different from a ticketing system?"** A ticket system records what people decide. PlumeProof decides *what to investigate next* from the evidence, and updates probabilities with each finding. It also enforces a lifecycle in which a ticket literally cannot be closed without a verified reduction, and it reopens itself on new satellite data.

8. **"Why Bayesian, not machine learning?"** We have no labelled dataset of resolved landfill investigations yet, and decisions here must be explainable to regulators. Bayes' rule with explicit likelihood tables is transparent, auditable and works from expert estimates. Every closed case becomes labelled data that could calibrate or learn those likelihoods later.

9. **"What if the true source isn't one of your three hypotheses?"** Then the beliefs can't converge properly, and verification catches it: a repair of the attributed source won't produce a verified reduction, so the case goes STILL_LEAKING, the agent down-weights that source and re-plans. In production we'd add an explicit "other / unknown" hypothesis. The three come from our proposal.

10. **"How do you validate the likelihoods?"** Honestly: not yet. They are illustrative values chosen to reproduce our proposal's worked example, within 0.5 percentage points. We show robustness instead: with ±20% perturbation the top-ranked action never changes (100% of 200 draws) and false closures stay at 0. Calibration with an environmental engineer, using case outcomes, is the next step.

## 5. Do not say

- ❌ "We reduced real methane emissions." The follow-up measurement and repair are **simulated**.
- ❌ "The likelihoods / costs are calibrated" or "validated by experts." They are **illustrative**.
- ❌ "Carbon Mapper confirmed the repair" or "satellites verified the reduction." Only the 2025/2026 plumes are real.
- ❌ "BMC / MPCB / the operator responded" or "is using this." The stakeholder mapping and responses are simulated.
- ❌ "Proven in the field" or "tested on 25 real cases." The 25 scenarios are **simulated**.
- ❌ "Zero false closures guaranteed in the real world." It holds in our simulation under a bounded-noise assumption.
- ❌ "The AI decides" or "the LLM closes cases." All decisions are deterministic code plus human approval; the LLM only drafts text.
- ❌ "The MQ-4 sensor verifies emissions." It is uncalibrated and not used for verification.
- ❌ "We use LangGraph / PostGIS." We use a hand-written orchestrator and SQLite; those are future work.
- ❌ Exact plume coordinates or a wind measurement. Positions are approximate and the wind arrow is illustrative.
